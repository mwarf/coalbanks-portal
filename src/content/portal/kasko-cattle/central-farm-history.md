## What the Museum Record Establishes

New research from the Gem of the West Museum adds an important layer to the historical setting around Kasko Cattle's Coaldale operations.

The historic **Central Farm** was developed by the Canadian Pacific Railway between 1916 and 1918 as the master farm of the **Van Horne Colony**. Located four miles north of Coaldale on all of Section 34, Township 9, Range 20, West of the Fourth Meridian, the roughly 600-acre operation was crossed by the Cameron Canal. It served as both a working farm and a demonstration centre for the surrounding irrigation district. (J. Gerald Snow, “The Central Farm — A Coaldale Landmark,” *Coaldale: Gem of the West, 1900–1983*, pp. 27–28.)

After the First World War, returning veterans purchased many nearby 160-acre colony farms with assistance from the CPR and the federal government. Central Farm supplied a working model of irrigated agriculture and helped settlers learning to farm in southern Alberta. The broader history is one of settlement, irrigation, agricultural demonstration, and land development—not solely a federal soldier-settlement project. (Snow, pp. 28–29.)

## Agricultural Legacy

Central Farm became known for livestock breeding and practical agricultural leadership. Its programs included Percheron horses, Shorthorn and later Aberdeen Angus cattle, Suffolk sheep, and Berkshire and Yorkshire hogs. The farm also hosted public demonstrations and field days and earned recognition at irrigation exhibitions. (Snow, pp. 29–31.)

Sugar beets were first planted there on ten acres in 1926 and later became a major part of the operation. Beef feeding also expanded over time: later facilities handled approximately 350 cattle annually before growing toward about 1,000 cattle moving through the farm each year. (Snow, pp. 31–34.)

## Ownership and Naming

| Period | Historical name or ownership |
|---|---|
| **1916–1945** | CPR Central Farm of the Van Horne Colony |
| **1945–1961** | Thomas G. Wood, Frank R. Taylor, and J. Gerald Snow |
| **1961–1978** | Central Farms Ltd. |
| **1978–at least 1983** | Unger–Van Ryn ownership; renamed Coaldale Cameron Farm |

The names matter. **Central Farm** refers to the original CPR operation; **Central Farms Ltd.** was the company incorporated in 1961; and **Coaldale Cameron Farm** was adopted after the 1978 sale. These historical operations are distinct from the modern Central Farms RNG project. (Snow, pp. 33–35.)

## How This Relates to the Kasko Story

The research strengthens the film's wider theme: Kasko Cattle operates within a Coaldale agricultural landscape shaped for more than a century by irrigation, livestock feeding, agricultural experimentation, and the resilience of successive farm families.

One detail still requires confirmation before it is presented as a direct title-history claim. The museum history places the original Central Farm on **Section 34**, while current Kasko research identifies the Home Lot at **SW 27-9-20-W4** and records its purchase from the Thiessen operation in 2001. These are adjoining sections in the same historic farm landscape, but the available evidence does **not yet prove** that the present Home Lot parcel was itself part of the original Central Farm.

For the film, the verified wording is:

> The Kasko Home Lot sits within the same historic agricultural landscape as the CPR's Central Farm and Van Horne Colony, where irrigation, livestock production, and agricultural settlement took root north of Coaldale beginning in the First World War era.
